# Stellata: Definitions

Each word in `STELLATA.md`, in up to three lines: its image, where the word comes from; its meaning in the principle; and its test, what decides it. This file adds to `STELLATA.md`, which stands without it: where the principle already gives a meaning, only the image and the test are here. No word names a project; how the principle is applied is in each project's own documents. A draft, changed as the principle is worked out.

The method is three questions, asked of a thing: what its center is (The hall), and what holds it and what it holds (Section). Each part found is a thing in turn (Relative), asked the same three.

## Stellata

- **Image:** the star-shaped flowers of *Magnolia stellata*.
- **Meaning:** the name of the form; the name chosen is arbitrary.
- **Test:** another name would change no test's answer.

## Rotonda

### Rotonda

- **Image:** Palladio's Villa La Rotonda, near Vicenza: a square building with a round, domed hall at its middle.
- **Meaning:** a thing seen from above: one center, and the parts around it.
- **Test:** the thing has one center, and each part around it is named by how it relates to that center.

### The hall

- **Image:** the domed room at the middle, which every portico leads into.
- **Meaning:** the center of a thing: what the thing is, and what its other parts are there for.
- **Test:** the center is said in one sentence; a part that serves none of it is not part of the thing.

### A portico

- **Image:** a porch of columns on one side of a building, its way in; La Rotonda has four, identical.
- **Meaning:** a way in: what passes between the hall and what lies outside it passes through a portico. A thing has an inside, the hall with its porticos, and an outside, what lies beyond them. The porticos are alike in form, and each is fitted to what lies on its side; none ranks above another. They are related by standing around the same center, not by joining each other: they meet only in the hall.
- **Test:** what lies outside reaches the hall only through a portico.

### The front

- **Image:** the side a building is entered from and shown by. La Rotonda has none: its four sides are alike, and it could stand anywhere; its site is one setting among many.
- **Meaning:** a thing has no front of its own; where it stands decides which side faces what. No side is the main one, and the center is the same in any setting.
- **Test:** the center names no side and no setting; sides of the same kind do not name each other; the thing can be placed in another setting without its center changing.

## Pavilion

### Pavilion

- **Meaning:** a freestanding structure, seen through its levels: what it holds, and what holds it.
- **Test:** each part is a three, a six or a nine.

### Three

- **Image:** a room.
- **Test:** it is made and checked alone, with nothing that holds it present.

### Six

- **Image:** a floor, which holds its rooms side by side.
- **Test:** the threes it holds pass the Three test without it.

### Nine

- **Image:** the building, which holds its floors and reaches each room only through a floor.
- **Test:** it names its sixes, never a three directly.

### Relative

- **Image:** step closer and one room is all you see; step back and the building is one of a town's.
- **Meaning:** a level is never what a part is on its own, only where it is relative to a thing. The center is a place in the plan and a level a place in the section, and neither fixes the other: the center has a level only relative to the thing it is seen from.
- **Test:** a level is never said alone, only relative to a thing ("a three relative to X").

## Plan and section

### Plan and section

- **Meaning:** a thing is what both drawings are cut from; each shows one direction of it, and neither is the thing. A drawing shows a thing as it is at one time; a change is a new drawing.

### Plan

- **Image:** a drawing seen from above, cut at one height, with the rooms side by side.
- **Test:** each part has one role, and parts with the same role are kept together.

### Section

- **Image:** a drawing cut upright through a building, with its levels one above another.
- **Test:** what holds is made of what it holds; what is held is complete without what holds it. Each part is held by one thing; a part reached from more than one side is related across, in the plan, not held twice.
