# Form

How the principle is used on a project's files and folders, at every size: what is asked at the start, and what is written as the project finds its form. It follows from `STELLATA.md`, with its words as `STELLATA_DEFINITIONS.md` defines them; each rule names the word it follows from. How a part is written is in `CONVENTIONS.md`, how the work is done in `COLLABORATION.md`.

## 1. Growing

Form is written when it is known, not before.

- Each new file or folder is asked the method's three questions: what its center is, what holds it, and what it holds. "Not settled yet" is an answer.
- A file states its one job in its first line.
- When the project's parts are settled, `docs/03-architecture.md` § 1 names each top-level part, its folder, and the principle's word for it.
- When a folder holds more than one part, it gets a readme (§ 2).

## 2. Readmes

A readme says first, in plain words:

- for a part: what it does, what it needs from around it, and what it gives;
- for a folder: what belongs in it, and the rules for what is in it, such as what it must not import.

Then it names the principle's word for it, what that word is relative to (Relative), what holds it, and what it holds. A word is used only as the definitions define it; a part no word fits is named as such, not given a new word. Names in the code stay plain.

## 3. Rules

Each holds at every size, from a file to the whole.

- Each thing has one center; a part that serves none of it is not part of the thing (The hall).
- What lies outside reaches a part only through its ways in, and the ways in meet only in it (A portico).
- The center names no side and no setting (The front).
- Each part is a three, a six or a nine, relative to the thing it is in (Pavilion, Relative).
- Each part has one role, and parts with the same role are kept together (Plan).
- What is held is complete without what holds it, and a thing is named for what it holds (Section).
- What serves the work on the thing, such as its tools, is not part of it, and is named as such (The hall).
